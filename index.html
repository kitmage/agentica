Namesake

USS Princeton is named for the American victories over the British at the Battle of Princeton, New Jersey, on 3 January 1777, when General George Washington’s Continental Army turned the tide of the Revolutionary War. She is the sixth U.S. Navy ship to bear the name Princeton.

Weapons & Sensors

Aegis Combat System

  • Princeton was the first Ticonderoga-class cruiser fitted with the upgraded AN/SPY-1B radar — a major leap in detection and resistance to enemy electronic countermeasures.
  • Paired with the Mk 99 Fire Control System to guide Standard Missiles against aircraft and missiles at extended range.

Armament

  • 122-cell Mk 41 Vertical Launching System — Tomahawk, Standard, ESSM and ASROC missiles
  • Two Mk 45 5-inch/62 caliber naval guns
  • Phalanx close-in weapon systems
  • Two MH-60R Seahawk helicopters

Service History

1989

Commissioned 11 February at Ingalls Shipbuilding, Pascagoula, Mississippi. Transited the Panama Canal to her first homeport, Long Beach, California.

1990

Served as flagship for the first U.S. Navy visit to the Soviet Pacific port of Vladivostok since before World War II.

1991 — Desert Storm

On 18 February, while operating in the northern Persian Gulf, Princeton struck two Iraqi influence mines — cracking her superstructure and jamming her port rudder. Her crew fought the damage, restored the ship, and remained on station. She earned the Combat Action Ribbon.

1998 – 1999

Deployed in support of Operation Desert Fox, striking Iraqi targets.

2001 – 2002

Fired Tomahawk cruise missiles in the opening strikes of Operation Enduring Freedom.

2003

Deployed for Operation Iraqi Freedom, providing air defense and strike support in the Arabian Gulf.

Today

Homeported in San Diego, Princeton remains the oldest Ticonderoga-class cruiser still in active commissioned service — over 35 years of Honor and Glory.

“She was built to fight, damaged in battle, repaired by her crew, and sent back to the fight — the very definition of Honor and Glory.”
